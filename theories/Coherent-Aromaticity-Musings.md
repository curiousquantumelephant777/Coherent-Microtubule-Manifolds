# ✨ Coherent Aromaticity Musings: 
## 
- I've been fascinated by how many psychedelic compounds have aromatic rings. In chemistry, aromaticity is defined by an atom having $4\pi + 2$ electrons (Hückel's rule) [1]. Trace amines have an amine group and an aromatic phenol. Aromatic rings are also characterized by coherent $\pi$ bond electron clouds.
- Certain substitutions seem to induce a different conformational coherent vibration. For instance, some phenol rings substituted by methelenedioxy- bridges or bromine/para-methyl groups seem to produce a highly empathogenic vibration (like some parts of Williams syndrome's social phenotype)
- I propose that indole rings induce a different vibrational mode defined by more intense experiences and sensory overload (the "ASD-like" phenotype).
- I've also been exploring piezoelectricity and how it relates to endogenous structural scaffolds (e.g., collagen and pineal gland structures). Piezoelectric material (like quartz) generates charge (voltage) from vibrational stimulation.
- Interestingly, the phenethylamines I've been modeling (see 2CB repo /src for the 2cb one) and the tryptamines seem to have another common theme: they visually look like a lock and key. I think this is not just a metaphor - the pi-bond clouds might lock into the receptors and induce some vibrational change, which generates an electromagnetic field, while the other edge of the key (amine group) keeps the coherent system locked into place by binding to the aspartate residues. I'm also exploring how certain artificial compounds like styrene and aspartame might tune these receptors to a distorted, decoherent version of the "aromatic psychedelic symphony." Question: Could psychedelics (especially iboga) can help re-tune these receptors back to their default state of coherence, via epigenetic mechanisms? I view this as a parallel computing system where these coherent compounds induce profound change, binding to receptors in parallel and exploring multiple paths simultaneously, which moves beyond the view of traditional linear neurochemical computing (e.g., one receptor cascade). Ibogaine's unique stable confirmation (which shares the indole ring but deviates from the traditional structure of tryptamines and phenethylamines) is especially fascinating, as it seems to affect multiple receptors in parallel and act as a "device firmware upgrade" which restores the coherent childhood OS with adult lucidity preserved.
